Why the bike should wear a Dutch plate.

Three reasons, all about paperwork:

1. **Carnet.** Iran, Pakistan, India and Nepal all require a Carnet de Passages.
   A Dutch-plated bike gets one through ADAC (discounted for ANWB members,
   deposit scaled to bike value and countries). Thailand doesn't issue them, and
   Thai temporary export permits run in 30-day blocks with extensions approved
   in advance.

2. **Buying.** Foreigners can own a bike in Thailand, but the DLT wants a
   certificate of residence, which means sorting a non-immigrant or DTV visa
   first, then a green book transfer.

3. **End state.** A Dutch bike in Thailand can be ridden on temporary import and
   shipped home or parked. A Thai bike in Rotterdam has no EU type approval, so
   registering it is somewhere between costly and impossible. You'd finish the
   trip holding a paperweight.
